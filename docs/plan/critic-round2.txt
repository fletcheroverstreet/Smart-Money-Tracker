=== data-backend  score 7
Round 2, data correctness and backend lens. The system is much better than in round 1. Every headline aggregate I recomputed with independent SQL now matches the API: /api/tickers at 365 days sorted by buys and by sells (top 10), /api/feed/today, and the stats of three profiles (Feiger, Tim Cook, OrbiMed). The round-1 examples are all fixed: the CRWV options, the FITB 4/A, the ETRA, Blackstone and Medline sibling filings, SVRE's ADS value, the Colgrove and Omnadora names, and the NA ticker. 38 random XML-loaded rows (P, S, A, M, F, D, J, C codes; 4/A; joint filings of up to 7 owners; derivatives) match the raw XML field by field. The only difference is a date written with a timezone suffix, which is normalised correctly. Tests (416 passed), ruff and mypy are clean, and the endpoints answer in under 90 ms.

Real problems remain in the rules themselves, and in one case they produce misleading output:
(1) The gap filler and /api/meta/coverage treat a day as loaded if any Form 3/4/5 exists that day. Sep 3, 4, 14 and 15 hold only Form 3s that the Atom poller picked up, and zero Form 4s. Neither the filler nor the coverage note will ever flag them, and all four days fall inside the 30-day window.
(2) The amendment_unpaired flag drops 50 genuine added rows ($107.5M, in 29 amendments) from totals.
(3) The duplicate rule merges unrelated direct holders who made identical IPO purchases (54 rows, $19.8M). It misses the same owner re-filing a trade weeks later (69 pairs). It also runs only every 30 minutes, so the Today strip can double count in between.
(4) The ADS check misses ratio footnotes: 94 rows, $110M overstated (51Talk 60:1, Burning Rock and Mesoblast 10:1).
(5) Kind and name rules still misfile Brazilian 'de' names and the surname Holding (Frank B. Holding Jr.) as companies. O'/D' surnames and compound surnames are garbled (about 7% of a 45-person sample).
(6) Placeholder tickers '[NONE]' and '[NONE' still rank on /tickers.
(7) Smaller issues: 34 derivative rows show unflagged values of $100M or more; a superseded Form 144 keeps its match; the watchdog misses transient give-ups; reprocess wipes the quality flags until the next quality job (up to 30 minutes); server-side rendering bypasses the rate limiter; latency under peak load is not yet demonstrated.
