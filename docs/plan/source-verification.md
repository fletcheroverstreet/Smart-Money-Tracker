# Source verification: evidence

Raw findings from the live verification on 2026-09-23. This is the working evidence behind PLAN.md §2.4. Each finding lists the spec claim, its status, what was observed (status codes, headers, element names), the design implication, and URLs. Findings were produced by research agents, then re-checked by an independent skeptic (section "Skeptic re-checks") and a completeness critic ("Known gaps"). ## edgar-plumbing

### [confirmed] 1. SEC fair-access policy (rate limit and User-Agent)
**Observed:** The /os/accessing-edgar-data URL returns 301 to /search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data (200, Last-Modified Wed 23 Sep 2026 21:56:04 GMT, page dated March 23, 2021). That page says: 'Fair access Current max request rate: 10 requests/second'. It asks bots to declare themselves with the sample header 'User-Agent: Sample Company Name AdminContact@<sample company domain>.com' plus Accept-Encoding: gzip, deflate and Host: www.sec.gov. /privacy.htm returns 301 to /about/privacy-information (Last Reviewed Nov 29, 2023), which says: no more than 10 requests per second in total 'regardless of the number of machines'; after a breach, access is limited until the rate stays under the threshold for 10 minutes; 'unclassified' bots are not allowed. /developer returns 301 to /about/developer-resources (Last Reviewed March 10, 2025) with the same 10 req/s wording. Tests on 2026-09-23: company_tickers.json with UA 'curl/8.0' returned 403 with a page titled 'Request Rate Threshold Exceeded', even though the rate was low. The Atom feed with UA 'Mozilla/5.0' returned 403 'Your Request Originates from an Undeclared Automated Tool'. With the declared UA 'finlake/0.1 (email)' every request returned 200. No 2025-2026 change to the limit was found.
**Implication:** Use one global token-bucket rate limiter shared across all EDGAR workers (www.sec.gov, data.sec.gov, efts.sec.gov), set well under 10 req/s (for example 5-8). Put a declared 'AppName contact@domain' UA on every request and send Accept-Encoding gzip. Treat any 403 as a UA or throttle problem, not as 'not found'; the 403 page title is misleading, so detect it by status code plus body. After a 403, back off for more than 10 minutes.
**Evidence:** https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data https://www.sec.gov/about/privacy-information https://www.sec.gov/about/developer-resources

### [confirmed] 2c. Atom feed: count, paging, depth, prefix matching
**Observed:** count=200 returned 100 entries, so 100 is the maximum. With no count the default is 40. start= paging works: start=100 returned the next 100 (17:57:56 back to 17:10:48 ET). type=4 with owner=include IS a prefix match: page 2 contained 424B2 (31), 497 (8), 485APOS (4), 425 (4), 424B5 (3), 424B3 (1) and 40-17G (1), all with the Filer or Subject role. Page 1 happened to be all 4 and 4/A. With owner=only, pages returned only 4 and 4/A. Depth with type=4&owner=only: start=1000 reached 2026-09-21 18:32 ET; start=2100 returned 92 entries, the oldest 2026-09-21T06:02:35; start=2200, 2400, 2500, 2800 and 3000 returned 200 with the title '... - No recent filings' and 0 entries. So the feed held about 2,192 Form 4 entries, covering roughly the current plus 2 prior business days (Mon to Wed evening). start=6000 once returned 503 from AkamaiNetStorage (an SEC maintenance page, 'Modified: Dec. 04, 2025').
**Implication:** Always filter by exact category term on the client; never trust type=. Use owner=only for the Forms 3/4/5 poll. Page with count=100 and increasing start until the entries overlap the last-seen accession. The window is only about 2-3 business days, so a longer outage needs daily-index backfill. Treat an empty feed titled 'No recent filings' as the end of the window, not an error. Retry on 503.
**Evidence:** https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcurrent&type=4&owner=only&count=100&start=2100&output=atom

### [partially_confirmed] 3. EDGAR filing hours and dating of late filings
**Observed:** eCFR 17 CFR 232.13 (fetched live by a sibling agent on 2026-09-23): under (a)(2), filings started after 5:30 p.m. ET are deemed filed the next business day. Under (a)(4), a Form 3, 4 or 5, Schedule 14N, Form 144, or Schedule 13D or 13G (including amendments) started on or before 10 p.m. ET is deemed filed the same business day. Live data agrees. BRK Form 4 0001193125-26-397059: acceptanceDateTime 2026-09-22T01:31:10.000Z (21:31 EDT 9/21), filingDate 2026-09-21. BRK SCHEDULE 13G 0001193125-26-352904: accepted 2026-08-15T00:18:26Z (20:18 EDT 8/14), filingDate 2026-08-14. BRK 11-K 0001193125-26-271365: accepted 2026-06-15T21:52:14Z (17:52 EDT), filingDate 2026-06-16 (next day). Form 4 0001213900-26-098884 has SGML ACCEPTANCE-DATETIME 20260910173029 (ET, no zone) and FILED AS OF DATE 20260910. The 06:00 opening time was not verified from an official page; the earliest observed Form 4 in the feed was 06:02:35 ET.
**Implication:** Store both acceptance_datetime (UTC) and filing_date, and never derive one from the other. For Forms 3/4/5/144/13D/13G, accepted from 17:30 to 22:00 ET still means the same filing date. For other forms (13F-HR), acceptance after 17:30 moves the filing date to the next business day. The submissions API acceptanceDateTime ending in 'Z' is real UTC. The SGML header and index.json 'last-modified' values are ET with no zone. Normalize all three.

### Risks raised
- The Atom type= filter is a prefix match. type=4 with owner=include returned 424B2, 497, 485APOS, 425 and 40-17G. Without exact client-side filtering the ingester would store prospectuses as insider trades.
- The Atom feed window is only about 2,190 Form 4 entries (about 2-3 business days; start>=2200 returns an empty 'No recent filings' feed). A poller outage longer than that loses data unless daily-index reconciliation exists.
- The legacy form type 'SC 13D' now returns an empty feed, a silent failure. Other SC-prefixed forms (SC TO-I, SC 13E3, SC 14D9) still exist, so a blanket 'SC ' to 'SCHEDULE ' rename would be wrong.
- Timestamps come in three zone conventions. Atom uses an explicit -04:00/-05:00 offset. The submissions API acceptanceDateTime is UTC with Z. The SGML ACCEPTANCE-DATETIME, index.json 'last-modified' and daily-index listing times are naive ET. Mixing them creates up to 5-hour errors and wrong trade-date buckets.
- The filing-date rule differs by form: 22:00 ET cutoff for 3/4/5/144/13D/13G, 17:30 ET for 13F and others. Deriving filing_date from acceptance time with one rule mislabels filings.
- The newest bulk zips moved from /files/structureddata/ to /files/datastandardsinnovation/ while older ones stayed. Hardcoded URL templates will break; scrape the landing pages.
- The insider-transactions readme PDF (Last-Modified 2022-08-24) does not document AFF10B5ONE, even though the landing page says it was added in July 2025. The schema must come from the zip metadata.
- Non-declared or browser-like UAs get 403 even at low rates, and the 403 page title 'Request Rate Threshold Exceeded' is misleading. There is also a 10-minute penalty box after a breach. All agents and services share one IP-level 10 req/s budget.
- One deep-paging request returned a 503 from Akamai with an SEC maintenance page (Modified Dec 04, 2025). The poller needs retry with backoff and must not treat 503 as end-of-feed.
- A form's primary XML filename is not standardized: Form 4 uses ownership.xml, form4.xml or wk-form4_<ts>.xml, and 13F information tables have arbitrary names. The submissions primaryDocument points to the XSL-rendered path (xslF345X06/...), not the raw XML.
- EFTS (efts.sec.gov) is not a documented public API. Its rate limit and lag were not measured, so building ingestion on it is fragile.

### Open questions raised
- Are you OK with polling-only near-real-time (Atom about every 30-60 s from 06:00 to 22:00 ET, plus nightly daily-index reconciliation)? The only push feed is PDS, which requires a paid Maximus subscription.
- What total SEC request budget should the app reserve (for example 5 req/s) for live polling vs. per-filing XML fetches vs. backfill? The 10 req/s cap is per IP across all processes.
- Should historical 13D/13G before Dec 2024 (legacy SC 13D/SC 13G, HTML or text, no structured XML) be in scope? It needs a separate parser or extraction approach.
- Is a roughly 100 MB-per-quarter 13F bulk download acceptable for backfill, or should 13F history be limited to a curated list of CIKs fetched per filing?

### [confirmed] 4. Filing deadlines and deemed execution date
**Observed:** Form 4 Instruction 1(a): due 'before the end of the second business day following the day on which a transaction ... has been executed', with a cross-reference to Rule 16a-3(g). Form 3 Instruction 2(a): 'within 10 days after the event by which the person becomes a reporting person' (form3.pdf Last-Modified 25 Aug 2026; SEC 1473 (03-26)). Form 5 Instruction 1(a): 'on or before the 45th day after the end of the issuer's fiscal year' (form5.pdf Last-Modified 25 Aug 2026). eCFR 17 CFR 240.16a-3 (as of 2026-09-21; last section amendment version 2023-02-27): under (g)(1), the Form 4 deadline is before the end of the second business day after execution. Under (g)(2), for a transaction under a 10b5-1(c) plan where the reporting person does not pick the execution date, the date the broker, dealer or plan administrator notifies the person is deemed the execution date. Under (g)(3), the same applies to discretionary transactions (16b-3(b)(1)) when the plan administrator notifies. The log truncated (g)(3) and anything after it, so I did not observe the cap on how late the deemed date can be. Business-day definition as observed: 17 CFR 240.13d-1 defines business day as 'any day, other than Saturday, Sunday, or a Federal holiday, from 12 a.m. to 11:59 p.m., Eastern Time' (for 13D/G purposes). Rule 0-3 lets a deadline that falls on a Saturday, Sunday or holiday roll to the next business day. Reg S-T 232.13(a)(2): a direct transmission that starts after 5:30 p.m. ET is generally deemed filed the next business day. I did not observe the Section 16 exception to that 5:30 p.m. cutoff (reportedly 10 p.m. for Forms 3/4/5).
**Implication:** Compute lateness against a federal-holiday business-day calendar. When deemedExecutionDate is present, use it rather than transactionDate as the clock start. Compare using the SGML ACCEPTANCE-DATETIME (ET) against the filing-date cutoff. Label any 'late filer' metric as approximate until the 16a-3(g) cap and the Section 16 evening cutoff are verified. The eCFR full-text API requires Accept-Encoding compression (without it you get error supportCode 11).

### Risks raised
- Schema versions differ by form and over time (X0607 for Form 3, X0609 for Forms 4/5, X0508 in 2025 filings). A parser pinned to one version will break silently.
- Booleans come as true/false or 1/0 in different filings. The XSD comment header is stale (X0304), and the official sample XML has a typo (rptOwnerCounrty), so do not build parsers from the samples.
- Filer data errors exist in real filings, e.g. the SEPN Form 4 codes S rows as Acquired (A), and the FULT Form 3 has noSecuritiesOwned=0 while reporting no securities. Validate and flag these rather than trusting them.
- Joint filings plus sibling filings for affiliated entities (DELL F26/F27; limits of 10 owners and 30 rows) cause double-counting unless trades are deduplicated across accessions and owners.
- A Form 4/A has no link to its original accession (only dateOfOriginalSubmission), and amendments re-report only the changed lines, so amendment merging is heuristic.
- Form 144 uses a different namespace, the address prefix varies (com: vs ns2:), dates are MM/DD/YYYY, and the filer is the seller, but the accession prefix belongs to a filing agent.
- EFTS returned an 'Internal server error' on a broad query and needs %20 query encoding. It is not reliable enough for primary ingestion.
- The Form 144 tech-spec page still labels the spec 'Version 2.0 (Oct 2022)', but the zip contains 'Version 3, March 2025'. SEC page metadata can lag the real documents.
- The Rule 16a-3(g) deemed-execution cap and the Section 16 evening filing cutoff were not observed, so any lateness metric built now could be wrong at the edges.

### Risks raised
- Legal: the agreement cites 5 U.S.C. app. 105(c), which bars obtaining or using reports for any commercial purpose other than news and communications media disseminating to the general public, with civil penalties up to $10,000. A commercial 'Smart Money Tracker' may not qualify for that exception.
- Akamai edge blocks by IP/geo: several 2026 scrapers report 403 or a 200-status 'Access Denied' page (errors.edgesuite.net Reference #) from VPS, datacenter and non-US hosts. The production host must be tested, and a status-code check alone will not catch the block.
- Akamai returns a static 503 'WEBSITE TEMPORARILY UNAVAILABLE DUE TO MAINTENANCE' page (Server: AkamaiNetStorage) for some requests. The poller must not read it as zero filings.
- There is no robots.txt and no documented rate limit, so self-throttle (0.25-0.5 s between requests is what working scrapers use) and back off on 403, 429 and 5xx.
- Amendments are separate UUIDs with no link to the original and repeat full tables, so naive ingestion double-counts trades.
- Transaction dates can precede filing by more than a year (the Exchange example: 2025-04-30 trade filed 2026-08-08). Signals keyed on filing date vs trade date will differ.
- About 32% of electronic rows have no ticker (bonds, munis, notes, private stock), and tickers appear in inconsistent forms (BRK.B / BRK-B, two-ticker Exchange cells).
- Paper filings (about 14% of 2021-2026 PTRs in one index) cannot be parsed without OCR.
- GitHub Actions scheduled crons were reported (petrsebrle README) to fire unreliably since about February 2026, if Actions is used as the US-egress workaround.
- Only a 6-year retention window: reports drop off the site, so historical backfill must be archived by us.

### Risks raised
- No free provider checked allows public display. Yahoo, Finnhub, Massive, Tiingo, FMP and Alpha Vantage free tiers are all personal/internal use only, and Finnhub also bars sharing 'derived results'. A public Smart Money Tracker that shows quotes, charts or even computed returns built from these sources is in breach unless a redistribution licence is bought.
- Stooq now serves a JavaScript proof-of-work browser challenge instead of CSV, so the long-standing free, keyless end-of-day source is gone for automated use.
- Nasdaq Data Link blocks scripted requests with Incapsula (403), so it was not verifiable.
- Five Select Sector SPDRs (XLB, XLE, XLK, XLU, XLY) had 2:1 splits on 2025-12-05. Unadjusted series will show false -50% moves, and fund names were rebranded to 'State Street ...'.
- yfinance is exposed to Yahoo-side data gaps (e.g. issue #2964, missing 2026-08-28 session) and to curl_cffi version breakage (1.5.2 was a fix for curl_cffi>=0.16). The local install is yfinance 1.5.2 / curl_cffi 0.16.0, behind the latest 1.7.0 / 0.16.3.
- api.polygon.io is scheduled for phase-out after the Massive rebrand. It still answered on 2026-09-23 but should not be hard-coded.
- Finnhub's published Quote schema has no timestamp field, so quote freshness cannot be checked from the payload alone.
- Some provider sites (FMP via CloudFront, Finnhub/Tiingo pricing pages rendered by JS) block or hide content from plain curl, so automated terms monitoring is unreliable; licence terms need a manual review cadence.

### Open questions raised
- Will Smart Money Tracker be public? ### Risks raised
- USAspending ignores unknown or invalid search filters without an error (for example recipient_id), returns HTTP 200 with a broader result set, and reports the dropped filter only in a 'messages' array. The ingest has to check messages or it will attribute the wrong awards to a company.
- USAspending has several parent UEIs per company. Lockheed has two P-level parents with the same DUNS, and the parent_uei on a child profile (JSQTW5L2SSM1) points to an empty parent, while the funded parent is ZFN2JJXBLZT3.
- DoD contract obligations for roughly the last 3 months are near zero in USAspending (FY months 10-12 of 2026 show $0.05B, $0.02B and $0.0B against $27-69B earlier), consistent with DoD's reporting delay. A contracts overlay would show false 'no activity' for defense stocks unless we add a caveat.
- The LDA API moved permanently from lda.senate.gov to lda.gov (301). Anonymous access is limited to 15 requests/min, and the ToS requires a specific disclaimer and cited access date. LDA has no structured bill numbers and contains bad dates (dt_posted 1905 on a 1999 filing).
- The OGE disclosure index is an undocumented JSON endpoint behind a DataTables page. OGE destroys reports after 6-7 years, and some documents need an OGE Form 201 request, which counts as a form submission and needs the user's action.
- The SEC Form ADV monthly zip filenames change format month to month (ia09012026-registered.zip, ia08032026_1.zip, ia060126_0.zip), so the files have to be found by scraping the page.
- FEC DEMO_KEY reports X-Ratelimit-Limit 10, which is too low for anything past a smoke test.

### Open questions raised
- Will you register for an LDA.gov API key (free, at https://lda.gov/api/register/, 120/min vs 15/min anonymous)? Registering accepts the LDA API Terms of Service, so you would need to do it yourself.
- Will you get a free api.data.gov key for the FEC API? DEMO_KEY showed a limit of 10.
- Is a FINRA API credential acceptable if short interest or other Query API datasets need one? FINRA short interest, Query API auth and redistribution terms were not verified in this pass and need a follow-up check.
- For the OGE 278-T overlay, is it acceptable to cover only what the OGE JSON index exposes (mostly PAS/senior officials, often scanned PDFs), with no agency-level 278-Ts?
- For government contracts, should the app show a lag caveat on DoD data, or leave out the most recent ~90 days for DoD?

### [partially_confirmed] Caddy reverse_proxy SSE streaming config
**Observed:** Caddy docs reverse_proxy.md 'Streaming': 'By default, the proxy partially buffers the response for wire efficiency.' flush_interval: a negative value (typically -1) enables low-latency mode, which disables response buffering and flushes after every write, and does not cancel the backend request even if the client disconnects early. The option is IGNORED, and responses are flushed immediately anyway, when the response is Content-Type text/event-stream, or Content-Length is unknown, or both sides are HTTP/2 with unknown length and identity/absent Accept-Encoding. v2.11.4 source streaming.go flushInterval(): returns -1 when resCT == "text/event-stream" or res.ContentLength == -1 or isBidirectionalStream, else h.FlushInterval. The Caddyfile parser accepts an integer or duration for flush_interval. encode directive: the default match includes 'header Content-Type text/*' (so it covers text/event-stream), with minimum_length default 512. encode.go only decides to encode on the first write if len(p)>512 or Content-Length>512, and its FlushError flushes the encoder.
**Implication:** If the backend sends Content-Type: text/event-stream, Caddy 2.11 streams it with no extra config, so flush_interval -1 is redundant but harmless. Recommended Caddyfile: `handle /api/stream/* { reverse_proxy api:8000 { flush_interval -1 } }`, placed outside any `encode` block (or add an encode match that excludes text/event-stream). encode's default match covers text/* and could gzip the stream once an event exceeds 512 bytes. Add `X-Accel-Buffering: no` / `Cache-Control: no-cache` headers from the app for other proxies. Send SSE heartbeats, because the backend request is not cancelled when the client disconnects under -1 mode, so the app must detect disconnects itself (sse-starlette does this).
**Evidence:** https://raw.githubusercontent.com/caddyserver/website/master/src/docs/markdown/caddyfile/directives/reverse_proxy.md https://raw.githubusercontent.com/caddyserver/caddy/v2.11.4/modules/caddyhttp/reverseproxy/streaming.go https://raw.githubusercontent.com/caddyserver/website/master/src/docs/markdown/caddyfile/directives/encode.md

### [confirmed] PostgreSQL LISTEN/NOTIFY payload limit
**Observed:** PostgreSQL 18 docs (current) and devel docs sql-notify.html both say the payload 'must be shorter than 8000 bytes' in the default configuration, and advise storing large data in a table and sending the key. The notification queue is 8GB in a standard installation. If it fills, NOTIFY transactions fail at commit, and cleanup cannot happen while a session that has executed LISTEN stays in a long transaction.
**Implication:** Send only small JSON (entity type, id, accession number) via NOTIFY, and have listeners read the row. Keep the listener connection in autocommit and out of long transactions.
**Evidence:** https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-notify.html https://www.postgresql.org/docs/devel/sql-notify.html

### [confirmed] psycopg 3 async notifies usage
**Observed:** psycopg 3.3.6 docs: keep the connection in autocommit to send/receive notifications promptly. A plain blocking Connection also works. Receive via the Connection.notifies() generator or add_notify_handler(). Use only one of the two, since 3.2.10 using both raises a runtime warning. AsyncConnection.notifies(*, timeout: float|None=None, stop_after: int|None=None) -> AsyncGenerator[Notify] (timeout/stop_after added in 3.2; stop_after may yield more when several notifications arrive in one packet). Docs recommend a dedicated connection waiting on the socket for disconnect detection, rather than polling with SELECT 1. **Implication:** Run one dedicated AsyncConnection (autocommit=True, outside the pool) per API process: LISTEN, then loop `async for n in conn.notifies(timeout=30)` and fan out to SSE subscribers through an in-process broadcaster. Windows dev must use SelectorEventLoop, but Linux containers are unaffected. psycopg-binary bundles its own libpq, so the libpq 17 cancel fix depends on the bundled version (not checked).

## Known gaps (completeness critic)

Where each one is handled: most are covered by a verification step at the start of the phase that uses the source (PLAN.md §12).

- **The 'finlake local store' price source is in the catalog but no group checked it: what price series it holds, how far back, how fresh, and on which tickers.** The PriceProvider design treats the local store as the primary source. If it lacks intraday data, delisted tickers or recent days, the external fallback chain carries far more load than planned. Past incidents (tests wiping ~/.finlake) also show the store can disappear. *Check:* List the price tables or files in the ~/.finlake store and record the ticker count, earliest and latest dates, bar granularity and split/dividend adjustment. Also confirm the new backend can read it without writing to it, and that tests cannot touch it.
- **Senate: no one checked whether a new official PTR download or API exists for 2025-2026 (unverified).** It decides whether the Senate ingest scrapes eFD behind an agreement form or uses a supported feed. *Check:* Check efdsearch.senate.gov, the Senate Ethics and Secretary of the Senate sites, and 2025-2026 news or GitHub issues for a bulk download or API. Record the URL or confirm there is none.
- **Legal limits on using congressional disclosure data were not raised. 5 U.S.C. 13107 (Ethics in Government Act) and the eFD agreement ban use for commercial purposes, credit ratings and solicitation.** A public hub that shows House and Senate PTRs could break the terms the scraper accepts. That could affect monetization, alerts and redistribution. *Check:* Get the exact agreement text from the eFD landing page and the statute text. Record which product uses are allowed and add a compliance note to the spec.
- **How long disclosures stay published: reports are generally kept public for about 6 years and then removed. The House Stock Watcher backfill source is dead.** The history backfill must come from official sources that may already have dropped older years. Data already ingested may later disappear upstream, and the design needs a rule for keeping it. *Check:* Check the earliest year still downloadable from the House Clerk and from eFD. Check whether older PTR PDFs return 404. Decide on a retention policy for PTRs we hold after the source removes them.
- **FINRA equity short interest (twice monthly): Query API auth, file access and redistribution terms were never checked.** The short-interest overlay may need credentials, or may be barred from public display. *Check:* Read the FINRA developer docs for the equity short interest dataset: whether it needs auth, the rate limits, the terms of use on display, and the publication schedule. Test one unauthenticated fetch.
- **FEC endpoints for PAC contributions to candidates, and mapping FEC candidate IDs to bioguide IDs, were never checked.** The PAC-money overlay cannot be joined to legislators without a confirmed endpoint and ID bridge. *Check:* Call /v1/schedules/schedule_b or /v1/schedules/schedule_a (committee to candidate) and /v1/candidate/{id}/ with DEMO_KEY. Confirm that congress-legislators id.fec covers all current members.
- **Nasdaq Data Link free end-of-day data was never checked.** It is a possible free end-of-day fallback now that Stooq has changed. *Check:* Check whether any free equities end-of-day dataset still exists on Nasdaq Data Link and what its terms and limits are. If not, drop it from the catalog.
- **Stooq changed, yfinance is fragile, and FMP was only partly confirmed. As a result, the free end-of-day fallback chain is not settled.** If yfinance breaks, the charts have no confirmed free historical source that allows public display. *Check:* Build a table of each provider with its display rights, history depth, adjusted versus raw prices, and the key it needs. Pick the fallback order. Test Stooq's new access method or remove it.
- **No one checked how ticker, CUSIP and asset-name mapping is licensed or covered. That includes the SEC Official List of Section 13(f) Securities, CUSIP (CGS) display licensing, and free-text PTR asset names. OpenFIGI was only partly confirmed.** Joining 13F rows and congressional trades to prices depends on this mapping. Showing CUSIPs may also need a license. *Check:* Get the current quarterly 13(f) securities list from sec.gov. Check CGS terms for showing CUSIPs publicly. Measure the OpenFIGI match rate and the keyless and keyed rate limits on a sample of 200 PTR asset names.
- **Form 4/A has no confirmed structured link back to the original filing (partial). schemaVersion strings have changed.** Deduplication and amendment handling could double-count or fail to replace transactions. A strict schema check would reject newer filings. *Check:* Pull 20 recent 4/A filings and see whether dateOfOriginalSubmission alone is enough to match an original, or whether matching needs issuer, owner and transaction date. List every schemaVersion seen in the latest Insider Transactions data set.
- **index.json: the rule for finding the primary XML was only partly confirmed. The Atom feed was only partly checked for 13F-HR, 144 and SCHEDULE 13D/G.** The pollers may pick the XSL-rendered path, or miss form types entirely. *Check:* For 10 filings each of Form 4, 13F-HR, 144 and SCHEDULE 13D/G, fetch index.json and record the raw XML filename pattern. Fetch getcurrent with type set to each form and confirm entries come back.
- **EDGAR filing hours and the dating of late filings are only partly confirmed. That includes the 5:30pm vs 10pm cutoff for ownership forms, the next-business-day filing date, and holidays.** Lateness calculations and 'filed on' displays would be wrong, as would the scheduling of daily-index reconciliation. *Check:* Look up EDGAR Filer Manual Vol. II on acceptance hours and Rule 13(a)-? filing dates. Compare acceptanceDateTime with filingDate for Form 4s accepted between 17:30 and 22:00 ET.
- **Form 144 XML was only partly confirmed. PDS and other push mechanisms were only partly confirmed.** This affects the Form 144 parser and how fresh the data can get. *Check:* Fetch 5 recent Form 144 filings and record the primary document and XML root and elements. Confirm whether PDS needs a paid subscription.
- **13D/13G deadlines after the 2023 amendments, and SEC changes in 2025-2026 (13F threshold, FIGI, Rule 13f-2/Form SHO), were only partly confirmed.** These drive lateness flags and possible schema changes to 13F. *Check:* Read the 2023 adopting release summary for the 5-business-day 13D and 45-day 13G deadlines. Check sec.gov rulemaking for the status of Form SHO and any 13F proposals as of September 2026.
- **House yearly ZIP: conditional GET (ETag/Last-Modified), update cadence, amount strings, robots and rate limits were only partly confirmed. There is no plan for scanned PTRs.** This affects how often the poller runs, how many requests it wastes, and whether amount buckets parse fully. Scanned PDFs need OCR or manual review. *Check:* Do two HEAD/GET requests a day apart and compare the headers. Collect every distinct amount string, including spouse and 'Over $50,000,000' forms, from a year of electronic PTRs. Count scanned PTRs a year and decide between OCR and 'view original'.
- **Senate paper PTRs (/search/view/paper/) and robots.txt were only partly confirmed. No one tested bot challenges from datacenter IPs.** The scraper may work from a dev machine and then get blocked from the deployment host. *Check:* Run the httpx flow from the planned host or a cloud IP, check for challenge pages, and record the paper PTR page structure (whether it only has image links).
- **The LDA API host changed (lda.gov), and bill-number fields were only partly confirmed.** The old host may shut down. The lobbying-to-bill link may need free-text parsing. *Check:* Confirm the lda.gov base URL, the deprecation date for lda.senate.gov, and how auth tokens carry over. Sample 50 filings for how bill numbers appear in lobbying_activities.
- **USAspending spending_by_award by recipient, and its rate limits, were only partly confirmed.** This decides whether the contracts overlay can match issuers to recipients reliably. *Check:* POST spending_by_award with recipient_search_text for 5 large contractors and compare the results with recipient UEI and parent lookups. Note any 429 responses.
- **OGE 278-T publication and Form ADV/IAPD bulk data were only partly confirmed.** These are the executive-branch trades overlay and the adviser metadata. *Check:* Confirm whether 278-Ts are only available by request on the OGE site or searchable online, and the format. Confirm the IAPD bulk download URLs for SEC-registered and exempt reporting advisers.
- **The Finnhub WebSocket free tier was only partly confirmed.** Live quotes streamed over SSE depend on it. *Check:* Connect with a free key, subscribe to 5 US symbols, and record the message rate, the symbol cap and any reconnection limits.
- **Caddy SSE was only partly confirmed. TypeScript 7 changed, and its compatibility with Next.js 16 was not resolved.** Buffering would break live updates, and the toolchain choice would block the frontend build. *Check:* Run Caddy with reverse_proxy flush_interval -1 in front of a test SSE endpoint and check that events arrive immediately and idle timeouts do not cut the stream. Check the Next.js 16 docs for TS 7 support, or pin TS 5.x.
- **Licensing for the unitedstates/images portraits was only partly confirmed. The newly flagged congress-legislators fields that must not be shown need a list. Wikipedia text is CC BY-SA, which requires share-alike.** These are attribution and privacy obligations on public pages. *Check:* Check the repo README for the license and source of each image. List the bio fields to drop in the loader. Draft the attribution text for Wikipedia and Wikidata content.
- **No one sized the historical backfill against the SEC limit of 10 requests per second. There is also no market-holiday calendar or ET timezone rule.** Backfill time and database size were never estimated. Lateness and trading-day math need a calendar. *Check:* Estimate the number of filings per year for each form from the daily indexes, and calculate how long a backfill takes at 8 requests per second. Choose a holiday calendar source (for example exchange_calendars or pandas_market_calendars) and check it has Python 3.14 wheels.
- **Two related bills are only partly confirmed: other 2025-2026 bills or rules changing PTR timing or format, and H.R. 7008's effect if it is enacted.** The PTR schema and deadline logic may have to change within the project's lifetime. *Check:* Search Congress.gov for 119th-Congress bills that mention the STOCK Act or periodic transaction reports, and record their status. Set a recurring check on H.R. 7008's Senate status.
